# Vesta operating plan, 10 August 2026

## 1. The answer in three lines

Before changing any model, spend half a day looking at what the current pipeline does to your photo: the central 3:4 crop and the mask dilated twice are more likely candidates than the backbone for the disfigured torso, and none of the six research angles ruled them out.

Then measure bare FLUX.2-klein-4B with mflux on your machine, because it is the only Apache-2.0 chain verified at the licence file that fits in 16 GB (https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B/raw/main/LICENSE.md) and because on the task you need it beats all the specialised VTONs and also the 32B FLUX.2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11734v2, Table 3).

And know that you have two licence blocks, not one: `segformer_b2_clothes` too, the model you make the masks with today, is non-commercial by NVIDIA inheritance (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVlabs/SegFormer/master/LICENSE, sec. 3.3).

## 2. The licence problem

### What is blocked in your stack today

| Component | Verified licence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| CatVTON (weights + code + demo) | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zheng-Chong/CatVTON/edited/LICENSE) | blocked |
| `mattmdjaga/segformer_b2_clothes` | `license: other`, refers to the NVIDIA Source Code License for SegFormer, sec. 3.3: non-commercial use, "for research or evaluation purposes only" | blocked |
| Base weights `booksforcharlie/stable-diffusion-inpainting` | CreativeML OpenRAIL-M | commercial use allowed, but with use restrictions to pass on to the end user, and you are on a third-party mirror with 421 downloads because the original from runwayml answers 401 |
| `rembg` + u2netp | MIT code, weights licence not verified by any of the six angles | unknown, to be closed |
| dataset `mattmdjaga/human_parsing_dataset` | no declared licence | absence of a licence, that is worse than NC |

Replacing only CatVTON does not unblock you. The replacement of the mask has to be planned together, not afterwards.

### The only alternatives that are really usable commercially and that run on a base M4 16 GB

**Generation: FLUX.2-klein-4B, and that is it.**
Apache-2.0 read at the file, `gated: false`, 3.88 billion parameters, official fp8 build of 4.08 GB and community mflux q4 build of 4.62 GB (https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B, https://huggingface.co/api/models/Runpod/FLUX.2-klein-4B-mflux-4bit). MIT mflux runtime, alive, last push the day I write (https://github.com/mflux-community/mflux). The `mflux-generate-flux2-edit` command has `flux2-klein-4b` as default and already accepts multiple `--image-paths` and the LoRA paths.

**Segmentation: no clean, ready answer.** It has to be said without softening it.
- `facebook/sam2.1-hiera-*` is Apache-2.0 and Apple publishes the official Core ML conversions for your machine (https://huggingface.co/apple/coreml-sam2.1-large). But SAM segments on a geometric prompt, it does not return clothing classes: a layer on top is needed.
- SAM 3 (19 Nov 2025) adds the text prompt, so it removes the need for Grounding DINO, and the SAM License grants commercial use. Weights gated with manual approval (https://huggingface.co/facebook/sam3).
- MediaPipe Selfie Multiclass has an explicit "clothes" class and Apache-2.0 code, but it runs at 256x256 and nobody has verified the licence of the `.tflite` weights.
- MODNet is the only case where the licence explicitly covers the weights too: "The code, models, and demos in this repository ... are released under the Apache License 2.0" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZHKKKe/MODNet/master/README.md). But it is portrait matting, not garment parsing.

All the rest of the specialised VTON line is closed, and it is not only the licence of the repo: VITON-HD is CC BY-NC 4.0 "research purposes only" and Dress Code forbids commercial use **of derivative works** in so many words (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aimagelab/dress-code/main/LICENCE). That clause closes Leffa (`virtual_tryon_dc.pth`), DSR-Tryon and CatVTON without needing a lawyer. IDM-VTON, OOTDiffusion, StableVITON, FitDiT, MV-VTON, Mobile-VTON (CVPR 2026) and SIFT-VTON (5 August 2026) are all CC BY-NC-SA on VITON-HD. The latest release of the line is five days old and confirms the trend: the academic world will not solve the problem for you.

A note to keep an eye on: the Apache-2.0 try-on LoRA for klein-4B exists, but it is trained using `segformer_b2_clothes` for the masking. If you replace the segmenter, the LoRA works on masks different from the ones it was trained on.

## 3. What to try first

**Half a day of diagnostics on the pipeline you already have, before downloading any weights.** In order:

**Step 1, five minutes: look at the crop.**
`CatVTON/utils.py:350` does `resize_and_crop`, which is a **central crop to the 3:4 ratio** followed by a LANCZOS resize to 384x512. If your seated photo is not already 3:4 vertical, the pipeline is throwing away edges before segformer sees anything. Save the output of that function on your photo and open it. If the torso is already amputated there, the whole discussion about the mask and the backbone is the cure of the wrong symptom.

**Step 2, one hour: look at the mask overlaid on the photo.**
`mask_from_person.py` does `isin(seg, {4,7})` and then two passes of `ImageFilter.MaxFilter(9)`. Two kernel-9 dilations on a 384x512 image are aggressive, and the selected classes do not include the arms. A swollen mask that invades the shoulders and torso produces exactly the defect you describe. Overlay it in red at 50% and look at it: it costs an hour and can settle the question.

**Step 3, three minutes of computing: A/B on the checkpoint.**
`download_weights.py` downloads only `mix-48k-1024` and `server.py:72` passes `attn_ckpt_version="mix"`. The number at the end of the folder is the training resolution, confirmed by the paper: 16,000 steps at 512x384 for the single models, 48,000 steps at 1024x768 for the mix (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.15886, sec. 4.2). You run at 384x512 with the checkpoint trained at 1024. Download `vitonhd-16k-512`, change one string, regenerate the same photo. Two runs of 90 seconds.

Careful: 48k and 16k are **steps**, not images. The mix sees more data (~73K pairs from three datasets), so it is not guaranteed that vitonhd wins. It is an A/B, not a certainty.

**Why this first, and not the model change.** Because all the available evidence points upstream of the generator: the try-on LoRA that would be your replacement uses the same `segformer_b2_clothes` and lists among its known limits exactly your case, "Garment-part segmentation occasionally includes nearby objects (e.g. furniture the subject sits on) in the bottom crop" (https://huggingface.co/xocialize/tryon-FLUX.2-klein-4B-lora/raw/main/README.md, Known limitations section). If the defect comes from the mask or the crop, changing generator takes it along and will have cost you days.

**Right after, half a day: measure bare klein-4B.** `pip install mflux`, `mflux-generate-flux2-edit` with a person photo and a garment photo, a single prompt, time it. The LoRA is not needed: in the benchmark the generalist editors were evaluated **zero-shot, with a single unified prompt and without fine-tuning**, and klein won anyway. The LoRA of 273 images and 0 likes comes after, if needed.

## 4. The full ladder, by gain divided by effort

The seconds per image are relative to your current 90 s at 384x512. Where there is no measurement on your machine I say so.

| # | What changes | Work | Seconds/image | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inspection of the 3:4 crop in `resize_and_crop` | 5 min | 0 | none, it is only a save to file |
| 2 | Visual inspection of the mask, then tuning of MaxFilter and of the classes | 1-2 h | 0 | a mask that is too narrow leaves the old garment visible; iterate by looking |
| 3 | A/B `vitonhd-16k-512` against `mix-48k-1024` at 384x512 | 1 h | unchanged | none, you change one string and go back |
| 4 | `guidance_scale` from 2.5 to 1.0 | 30 min | about half: it disables the CFG branch and halves the UNet batch | loss of adherence to the conditioning, to be judged by eye |
| 5 | Tight crop on the mask bounding box, generate, recompose | 1 day | unchanged | 2-3x of useful pixels on the garment at equal compute; careful with the recomposition, see the note below |
| 6 | Crop of the garment to its alpha bounding box before padding | 1-2 h | 0 | none, you gain pixels on the print |
| 7 | MPS watermarks set **as a pair**, plus `torch.mps.empty_cache()` between models | 2-3 h | removes the swap degradation when present, otherwise 0 | with the threshold too low some loads that used to pass slowly now fail: a fallback is needed |
| 8 | Measurement of bare klein-4B with mflux, 384x512, multi-reference | half a day | unknown on your machine | the disk: see note |
| 9 | Pose gate with Apple Vision before spending the compute | 1 day | -90 s on the rejected photos | false rejections to tune |
| 10 | Switching the generator to klein-4B + prompt, keeping your masking | 3-5 days | unknown | on Model2Model klein collapses: see note |
| 11 | `xocialize` LoRA on top of klein-4B | +1-2 days | unchanged compared to 10 | LoRA dialect and order of the references not verified |
| 12 | Replacing segformer with SAM 2.1 Core ML or MediaPipe | weeks | unknown | it is the only way to remove the second licence block |
| 13 | Final upscaling (SwinIR, Apache-2.0) | 1 day | +a few seconds | cosmetic, it does not straighten a torso |

**Notes that change the entries.**

Entry 5 and recomposition: the idea of putting the original pixels back outside the mask is right for face and background, but it is not as free as it looks. The generated image is not registered with the original, because `resize_and_crop` did a central crop plus a downscale. To recompose at full resolution you have to invert both, and you bring the generated region back up from 384x512, that is you reintroduce blur exactly where you do not want it. Do it for face and background, do not expect magic on the garment.

Entry 7, and this is measured on your machine: `recommendedMaxWorkingSetSize` is 12,713,115,648 bytes, that is 11.84 GiB, 74.0% of the 16 GB. PyTorch MPS has factory high watermark 1.7 and low 1.4, so it lets you allocate up to 20.13 GB on a machine that has 16 before raising OOM: above 12 GB it is silent swap (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/v2.8.0/aten/src/ATen/mps/MPSAllocator.h). If you set only `PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO=1.0` **PyTorch does not start**: the low stays at 1.4 and the `low <= high` check fails. Both have to be set, and 1.0 is not an anti-swap guard but a ceiling at 11.84 GB with 4.2 GB left to macOS: if the goal is not to swap, the threshold is closer to 0.6-0.7. There is also a second, lower ceiling that nobody mentions: `maxBufferLength` = 9,534,832,640 bytes, that is 8.88 GiB per single allocation.

Entry 8 and the disk: the official fp8 build weighs 4.08 GB and the mflux q4 4.62 GB, but the full upstream repo is 23.72 GB and the mflux README warns of a download of about 15 GB. With the disk almost full, the difference between 4.6 and 15-24 GB decides whether the experiment starts. Check **first** that mflux accepts the already quantised repo on the edit path with LoRA.

Entry 10 and garment acquisition: on Shop2Model, that is an isolated garment photo plus a person photo, klein is the best of the study with 3.96/3.99/3.56 against 3.39/2.14/1.26 for CatVTON. On Model2Model, that is taking the garment from a photo of someone wearing it, klein collapses to 1.03 overall, worse than CatVTON at 2.25 (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11734v2, Tables 3 and 4). It is a product constraint: garments have to be acquired as isolated product shots, not cut out of outfit photos. The LoRA too demands the same format.

Entry 12: SAM alone does not return semantic clothing classes. The realistic road is SAM 3 with a text prompt, or MediaPipe for a coarse mask refined by SAM. Neither is a drop-in.

## 5. Cutout in the browser

**Technically it is realistic today, and there are measurements on your machine.** Chromium 148, WebGPU on Metal-3, base M4 under load:

| Model | Measured time | Download | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| IS-Net fp16, 1024x1024 | 561 ms at steady state, 997 ms the first, 4.9 s the initial load | 88 MB fp16 (176 MB fp32) | contaminated, see below |
| MODNet fp32, 672x512 | 328 ms WebGPU, 1160 ms WASM | 6.6 MB quantised, 13 MB fp16, 26 MB fp32 | explicit Apache-2.0 on the weights too |
| BiRefNet_lite | **does not run** | 224 MB | MIT on the repo, but contaminated |
| segformer_b2_clothes | **does not run** on WebGPU; 3.0 s in WASM with 128x128 output | 28.8 MB quantised | non-commercial |

The failure of BiRefNet and segformer in the browser is deterministic and does not depend on the load: `Too many storage buffers in shader. Current: 11, Max is 10`, because Apple GPUs expose `maxStorageBuffersPerShaderStage=10`. In WASM BiRefNet goes into `std::bad_alloc`. There is no public int8 version, and in any case int8 on Apple WebGPU costs 27-32 s against the 561 ms of fp16: on Apple you use fp16, never int8.

**The problem is the licence, and it is worse than it sounds.** IS-Net is trained on DIS5K, whose terms of use say: "Without permission from the original authors, commercial use of this dataset is prohibited even after copying, editing, processing or any operations of this database" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xuebinqin/DIS/main/DIS5K-Dataset-Terms-of-Use.pdf). The weights are a "processing" of that dataset. The Apache-2.0 tags of `BritishWerewolf/IS-Net` and the MIT of `imgly/isnet-general-onnx` are assertions by third parties who re-uploaded the rembg ONNX export and did not train anything. The same holds for the general-use weights of BiRefNet (DIS5K-TR training set in the README table) and for BEN2 ("trained on the DIS5k and our 22K proprietary segmentation dataset").

So, in order of what you can really ship:

1. **Quantised MODNet, 6.6 MB, Apache-2.0 on the weights, 328 ms measured.** It is the only clean and fast thing. But it is portrait matting: on a garment lying flat the edge quality has to be tried on your real photos before deciding, and nobody has done it.
2. **Write to Xuebin Qin for permission on DIS5K.** The terms themselves invite you to ask. If it comes, IS-Net fp16 at 561 ms and 88 MB is the technically best client-side solution that exists today on Apple.
3. **MediaPipe Image Segmenter**, same API on Python and on Web, but 256x256 and the weights licence not verified.

To avoid in the browser: RMBG-1.4 (BRIA proprietary contract for non-commercial use, not Creative Commons as you read around), RMBG-2.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0), and the `@imgly/background-removal` library which is pure AGPL-3.0 and would oblige you to publish the Vesta source, besides being stuck at 18 July 2025.

Product note: what the browser can do is the **garment cutout**, not the try-on. In 2026 there is no generation model that runs client-side at a usable speed. The static version can add garments to the wardrobe, not try them on.

## 6. What not to do

**iris.c.** It is the most seductive project of the survey (pure C, MIT, brilliant benchmarks) and it has been stopped since 13 February 2026, with 23 open issues. Issue #50 is an `EXC_BAD_ACCESS` from a use-after-free in the MPSGraph graph cache that starts from `iris_img2img` with reference images: opened on 23 March, zero comments. It is exactly the code path a multi-reference try-on would use. Moreover in the only independent comparison on the same machine it loses to mflux precisely on img2img: 86.3 s against 52.4 s at 1024x1024 (https://lilting.ch/en/articles/flux2-klein-4b-mflux-iris-m1-max).

**FLUX.2-klein-9B with the fal try-on LoRA.** It is the first thing you find searching, the LoRA has 4287 downloads and the Apache-2.0 badge in plain view, and the base model is `flux-non-commercial-license` (https://huggingface.co/api/models/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-9B). It is also 18.16 GB for the main file alone. The good variant is the 4B.

**Qwen-Image-Edit and all the 20B models.** Real Apache-2.0, but the MLX q4 build weighs 27.26 GB and mflux warns that Qwen below 6 bits degrades much more than Flux. On 16 GB it is not a road, it is a plan for when you have a server. The same goes for DSR-Tryon (58 GB), Step1X-Edit (52 GB, 257 downloads a month), HiDream-E1 (47 GB, abandoned), OmniGen2, LongCat.

**Raising the resolution to 512x768 or 768x1024.** CatVTON concatenates person and garment on `concat_dim=-2`, so the attention sequence at 384x512 is already 6144 tokens, not 3072, and it rises to 12288 and 24576. The cost goes with the square and you hit the indexing limits of Metal. The way to have more pixels on the garment is entry 5 of the ladder, the tight crop, which is free.

**`pipe.enable_attention_slicing()` and `enable_model_cpu_offload()` on Vesta.** `CatVTONPipeline` is a normal Python class, not a subclass of `DiffusionPipeline`: those calls give `AttributeError`. And the real equivalent, `unet.set_attention_slice`, installs `SlicedAttnProcessor` on every attention module and deletes the `SkipAttnProcessor` that CatVTON puts on the cross-attentions: since the pipeline calls the UNet with `encoder_hidden_states=None`, you get a crash, not saved memory.

**Forcing the VAE tiling.** The pipeline uses `stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse`, which has `sample_size=256`, so at 384x512 the tiling **already activates by itself**. The values you find around to force by hand are exactly the defaults of that VAE.

**`sudo sysctl iogpu.wired_limit_mb`.** The default already leaves only 4.2 GB to macOS. Wired memory cannot be reclaimed by Jetsam: you do not buy slow swap, you buy a kernel panic. All the guides that recommend it reason on 32, 64 and 128 GB.

**Core ML via `apple/ml-stable-diffusion` and DiffusionKit.** The first has been stopped since 3 July 2025, has benchmarks from 2023, does not support FLUX, and in Apple's own Mac benchmarks all the numbers are `CPU_AND_GPU`: the Neural Engine there is for iPhone and iPad. DiffusionKit has been archived since 11 April 2025. Core ML palettization has a useful data point to keep in the archive (at 4 bits per tensor Apple itself says "we are unable to get a good image anymore", while at 4 bits per channel group the quality comes back), but it would freeze you on the 2022 architecture that is the cause of the problem.

**CodeFormer and GFPGAN for the face.** The first is S-Lab License 1.0, non-commercial. The second opens the LICENSE file with Apache 2.0 and about two hundred lines later lists StyleGAN2 under a non-commercial NVIDIA licence and DFDNet under CC BY-NC-SA. And in any case it is the wrong cure: the face is ruined because it goes through the VAE, not because the model draws it badly.

**IP-Adapter and ControlNet on CatVTON.** The paper explicitly says to remove "redundant image and text encoders as well as cross-attentions": there are no cross-attention layers where IP-Adapter could inject. For ControlNet, besides the architecture work, the OpenPose annotator derives from the CMU weights, "ACADEMIC OR NON-PROFIT ORGANIZATION NONCOMMERCIAL RESEARCH USE ONLY".

**Real-ESRGAN.** Clean BSD licence, dead project: last real code commit on 20 September 2022, 585 open issues, and a clean installation still crashes with `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'torchvision.transforms.functional_tensor'`, with the fix PR not merged. If you need upscaling, SwinIR is Apache-2.0.

**`u2net_cloth_seg` as a replacement for segformer.** Repo `levindabhi/cloth-segmentation` archived since 17 April 2023, and the file weighs 176 MB against the 110 MB of the SegFormer it should replace. It is not a cost-neutral change, and nobody has verified the terms of iMaterialist 2019.

**Any2anyTryon.** No licence anywhere: you do not have permission even for research.

**TryOffDiff.** SSPL, that is network copyleft: if you offer it as a service you have to release the whole stack. Stopped for a year.

## 7. Numbers that do not hold

Things nobody managed to verify, or that were verified and turned out false. Listed because an honest "not verified" is worth more than a plausible number.

- **No speed measurement on a base MacBook Pro M4 16 GB with a 2026 model.** None, in six research angles. The only M4 16 GB measurements that exist are from 7 December 2024 on FLUX.1-schnell (2024 source, declared as such), and the community line "MacBook Pro M4, 19 s" in the iris.c README does not say which M4 or how much RAM.
- **The least bad proxy is 15 s per step on an M3 Pro 18 GB**, at 1024x1024 with 4-bit weights, that is about 60 s for a 4-step generation (https://github.com/mflux-community/mflux/issues/407). It is text-to-image, not multi-reference edit, and you work at a fifth of the pixels. The real number is unknown.
- **The "2-3x compared to the M1 Max" extrapolation does not hold.** It rests only on memory bandwidth, ignores that the M1 Max has 32 GPU cores and your M4 has 10, and is contradicted by the fact that the M4 Max turns out faster than the M1 Max on the same model. The error can go in both directions.
- **The 12 s at 512px and 21 s at 1024px of mflux on M1 Max are the same run measured in two ways.** 21.4 s is the pure inference at 1024, 31.7 s the wall time of the same run; the real 512 is 12.0 s / 23.7 s. They are 4-step text-to-image, not a three-image edit.
- **The `xocialize` LoRA is trained on `FLUX.2-klein-base-4B`, the NOT distilled model**, as the Training section of its card says, while the `base_model` metadata indicate the distilled one. The mflux README warns that the base models run in more steps. If the base is needed to make the LoRA behave well, every 4-step estimate disappears.
- **Whether mflux loads the "rank 32 (BFL-fused)" LoRA dialect.** The 4B + edit + LoRA path is wired in the CLI, but that specific dialect is not confirmed. First experiment, one hour.
- **The order of the three references.** The card says "Reference order (load-bearing): person, top, bottom", and nobody has verified that `--image-paths` passes them to the network in that order. If wrong, the LoRA runs and produces garbage, which is the worst way to fail because it looks like a quality problem.
- **The real quality of the LoRA.** 273 tuples, intermediate checkpoint 2000/3000, 144 downloads, 0 likes, no independent evaluation. If it does not hold on your photo, this angle has no second answer at the same size.
- **Which variant of klein was tested in VTEdit-Bench.** In the text of the paper neither "4B" nor "9B" ever appears. If it were the 9B, the result overestimates the 4B.
- **Whether BF16 on your M4 is hardware accelerated or emulated.** Secondary sources say that from M3 onwards it is native, but there is no Apple table that declares it for the M4 GPU. Your pipeline runs in bfloat16 and this is a ten-minute test that can be worth a factor of 2.
- **The licence of the u2netp weights used by rembg.** The code is MIT, the weights are distributed separately and nobody checked the terms. An open hole in your current stack.
- **The terms of the ATR / `human_parsing_dataset` dataset**, and those of iMaterialist 2019.
- **Whether a CC BY-NC dataset licence legally contaminates the trained weights.** It is a contested question. For Dress Code the theory is not needed, the ban on derivative works is written. For the others a lawyer decides, not a search.
- **The "40% faster than ComfyUI" of Draw Things.** It circulates on at least two blogs and in neither is there a measured time. The ANE on M4 is 1.8x declared by their engineering blog, not 2x, and only on the "8-bit S" quantised models. The chain you would need (headless server plus FLUX.2 Klein plus mask passed from outside) is not demonstrated by any of the available sources.
- **The on-device latency of Mobile-VTON.** The paper is titled "High-Fidelity On-Device Virtual Try-On" and does not publish a latency on any concrete device.
- **The limit of 10 storage buffers per shader stage on Apple GPUs** is a measurement made on your machine by querying the adapter, not a primary Apple source. Plausible and consistent with the absence of any client-side BiRefNet demo, but not corroborated.
- **All the browser measurements were taken with the machine under level 2 memory pressure and swap between 9 and 20 GB.** They are conservative upper bounds. This holds for any measurement you make now too: before timing klein-4B, free the machine, or the number you get is not comparable with the 90-second reference.
